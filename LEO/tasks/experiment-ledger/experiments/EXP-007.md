# EXP-007 — the legal design band costs ~4× in achievable affinity

**Date:** 2026-11-14 · **Verdict:** `worked` · **Theories:** TH-014, TH-009 · **Ideas:** IDEA-011, IDEA-008
**Literature check:** `skipped:external` — teammate's run, recorded retrospectively

> **Provenance.** Not our run. Branch `origin/nick`, commit `fcb238e`, file
> `NICCOLO/eda/AGENT_GUIDE.md`.
>
> **This entry corrects a number this ledger published.** TH-014, the standing
> review and the PR #3/#6 descriptions all quoted a top-20 median KD of
> **1.42 nM**. That figure is real but not achievable: its top 20 is full of 1–2
> substitution variants `abguard` forbids.

## What we expected

That the pre-filter's validated performance and its top-20 affinity hold inside
the band the identity rule actually permits. We had been quoting in-band figures
already — AUROC 0.915, Spearman +0.622 — so the band seemed accounted for.

## What actually happened

**First, the band itself was wrong.** We and the pre-filter had both used
**≥3 substitutions**, reasoning from `abguard`'s ">2 point mutations" rule. But
on a **10-residue** CDRH3, 3 substitutions leave 7/10 = **exactly 70%** identity,
and the rule requires *strictly under* 70%. So the minimum is **4**.

| Stage | Metric | Overall | ≥4 band | (previously quoted, ≥3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classifier | AUROC | 0.927 | **0.913** (n=556) | 0.915 |
| Ranker | Spearman | +0.687 | **+0.628** (n=131) | +0.622 |
| Ranker | top-20 median true KD | 1.42 nM | **6.5 nM** | — |

Set median across all 179 quantified variants is 86 nM.

**The model barely degrades; the constraint does the damage.** AUROC moves
0.927 → 0.913 and Spearman +0.687 → +0.628 — both negligible. But the achievable
affinity moves 1.42 nM → 6.5 nM, roughly **4.6×**. The guide's own summary:
*expect the legal band to cost you roughly 4× in achievable affinity before any
model is involved.*

## Why we think it happened

The tightest binders in SPR-768 are near-wild-type. Affinity maturation found
them by making one or two changes to an already-optimised paratope, and those
are precisely the variants the novelty rule excludes. The constraint is not
selecting against our model's blind spots — it is selecting against the region
of sequence space where the good binders actually live.

## What it implies for the theories

- **TH-014 corrected.** The in-band numbers become 0.913 and +0.628, and the
  top-20 median becomes **6.5 nM**, not 1.42 nM. The theory's claim survives —
  a sequence model does rank these variables well enough to choose submissions,
  and 6.5 nM is still ~13× better than the set median — but the ceiling is lower
  than we recorded. Confidence stays `high`.
- **TH-009 independently corroborated.** Our ≥4 figure came from an 11-residue
  CDRH3 (`WGGDGFYAMDY`, 4/11 = 63.6%); this one comes from the 10-mer identity
  arithmetic (3/10 = 70%, which fails). Two routes, same operative minimum. That
  the pre-filter was previously set to ≥3 is a reminder that the off-by-one
  warning in root `AGENTS.md` is live and has already caught two of us.
- **TH-018 (new, `high`).** The identity constraint costs ~4× in achievable
  affinity, and that cost is imposed by the rule rather than by any model.

## Why this matters for the pitch

Ranking is scored on improvement over measured trastuzumab, and this says
something uncomfortable and useful: the designs we are *permitted* to submit
start from a worse part of the affinity landscape than the dataset's best
variants occupy. Saying so explicitly — with 6.5 nM against 1.42 nM — is a
stronger judgment story than quoting the unconstrained number and hoping nobody
checks which variants it came from.

## What we would do differently

Check the arithmetic of a constraint before building a validation band on it.
Both the pre-filter and this ledger carried ≥3 for hours. The root `AGENTS.md`
off-by-one warning said to use the stricter reading, and neither of us applied
it to the identity rule — only to the mutation-count rule it was written about.
