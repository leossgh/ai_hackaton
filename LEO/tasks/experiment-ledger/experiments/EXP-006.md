# EXP-006 — does Protenix rank binders better than Boltz-2? No

**Date:** 2026-11-14 · **Verdict:** `failed` (the hypothesis was wrong) · **Theories:** TH-013 · **Idea:** IDEA-012
**Literature check:** `skipped:budget` — AMASS credits exhausted team-wide

> **This one is ours**, not a teammate's. First experiment in the ledger run by
> this task, and the first with its hypothesis written before the result.

## Hypothesis, stated before the run

Protenix separates measured HER2 binders from non-binders better than Boltz-2
does. `AGENTS.md` prefers folding models that take a template (OpenFold3,
Protenix, Boltz-2, Mol-1), so if EXP-004's failure were specific to Boltz-2,
switching model would rescue structure-based ranking.

This mattered because the alternative — that the failure is a property of the
*problem* — would close off an entire approach, and because the obvious next
move after EXP-004 is "try a different folder", which costs GPU time.

## What the literature said going in

Nothing new: AMASS credits are exhausted (HTTP 403), so step 2 of the cycle was
skipped as `skipped:budget`. Standing knowledge: TH-011 has AF3 at 2.9 Å median
CDR-H3 with 20 seeds, and EXP-003 found AF3 ipTM separates *shuffled* pairs at
0.754 while Boltz-2 manages 0.645.

## What we did

No new folding runs. The teammate committed a two-model calibration table
(`fd1b002`) with both Boltz-2 and Protenix scores against the same 60 SPR
variants — 30 with a measured KD spanning 0.023–13,863 nM, 30 with none — but
the scripts that would summarise it (`p8_rank_power.py`, `p9_protenix.py`) did
not commit their numeric output. So we computed the statistics ourselves:
rank-based Mann-Whitney AUROC with tie correction, and Spearman as Pearson on
ranks, in [`../analysis/px_calibration.py`](../analysis/px_calibration.py) with
no scipy dependency.

## What actually happened

| Metric | AUROC | Spearman (binders) |
|---|---|---|
| `p_binder` (sequence) | **0.980** ⚠️ in-sample | 0.830 ⚠️ |
| `px_iptm_BD` | 0.526 | +0.165 |
| `boltz2_iptm` | 0.502 | +0.140 |
| `px_iptm` | 0.498 | +0.237 |
| `px_rank` | 0.496 | +0.234 |
| `px_ptm` | 0.486 | +0.206 |
| `px_plddt` | 0.459 | +0.134 |
| `px_iptm_ab_her2` | 0.448 | +0.239 |
| `px_iptm_BC` | 0.446 | +0.200 |
| `px_gpde` | 0.426 | +0.213 |

**The hypothesis is wrong.** Every Protenix metric sits between 0.426 and 0.526
— chance — including `px_iptm_ab_her2`, the antibody↔HER2 chain-pair ipTM, which
is the most targeted interface score available and is *below* chance.

⚠️ **`p_binder`'s 0.980 is not a validation figure.** These 60 variants are part
of the classifier's own training data, so it is in-sample and inflated. The
honest grouped-CV numbers are AUROC 0.927 overall, 0.915 in the ≥3-mutation
band, and 0.849 on this subset (EXP-003, EXP-004). It is in the table only to
make the scale of the fold-metric failure legible. Quoting 0.980 anywhere would
be exactly the leak the teammate's own README warns about.

## Why we think it happened

EXP-004's mechanism generalises. All 60 complexes share 326 of 336 tokens, so a
*global* confidence score is dominated by the unchanged framework and HER2. But
that cannot explain `min_ipae` (spread 3.0) or `px_iptm_ab_her2` (a chain-pair
score that ignores the framework), and both fail too. The more likely reading is
that these models reproduce a plausible antibody–antigen geometry for any
CDRH3 sequence we hand them, because the pose is given by the template, and
their confidence reports geometric plausibility rather than binding.

## What it implies for the theories

- **TH-013 broadened from one model to the class.** Its claim was about "fold
  confidence"; the evidence was Boltz-2 and three models on shuffled pairs.
  Now a second template-taking model, scored against measured KD, fails
  identically. Confidence stays `high` — it was already there — but the scope
  is wider and better earned.
- **A line of enquiry closed cheaply.** Nobody needs to spend H100 time
  refolding with OpenFold3 or Mol-1 in the hope of a better screen. At 7.9 s per
  structure (EXP-004) that would have been tempting and would have taught us
  nothing.
- **IDEA-012 reinforced again.** The decoy/calibration control keeps paying.

## Honest limits

- n=30 binders, n=60 total. A weak effect is not excluded — the Spearmans are
  all faintly positive (+0.134 to +0.239), and EXP-004 found the same thing
  (all |Spearman| ≤ 0.21, p ≥ 0.28). With this sample, faint and absent are
  indistinguishable.
- Protenix was run by a teammate with settings we did not inspect. A different
  recycling or seed configuration might do better, though nothing suggests it.
- **Scope, as always:** fixed-framework CDRH3 optimisation. For de novo designs
  the whole binder changes and AF3 ipTM retains signal (0.754, EXP-003).

## What we would do differently

Nothing about the method. Worth noting for the pitch: this experiment cost two
minutes and no GPU because a teammate had already committed the raw table. The
cheapest experiment available is often re-analysing data someone already
produced — which is an argument for the repo scan being step A of every pass.
