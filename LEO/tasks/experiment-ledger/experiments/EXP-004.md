# EXP-004 — fold confidence calibrated against measured KD: it does not screen

**Date:** 2026-11-14 · **Verdict:** `worked` · **Theories:** TH-013, TH-011 · **Idea:** IDEA-012
**Literature check:** `skipped:external` — teammate's run, recorded retrospectively

> **Provenance.** Not our run. Branch `origin/nick`, commit `3dc1f24`, files
> `NICCOLO/eda/{make_calibration.py,p6_iptm_calib.py,p7_ipae.py}` and
> `figs/iptm_calibration_full.csv`.

## What we expected

That fold confidence adds screening power on top of the sequence pre-filter.
EXP-003 had shown structure scores separate real from *shuffled* pairs poorly,
but a shuffled pair is an easy negative. The open question was whether these
scores separate **real binders from real non-binders** — the actual task.

## What actually happened

60 SPR variants — 30 with a measured KD spanning **0.023–13,863 nM**, 30 with
no measurable KD, trastuzumab as control — folded with Boltz-2 against HER2
domain IV using the 1N8Z template.

| Metric | AUROC, binder vs non-binder |
|---|---|
| ipTM | 0.502 |
| pTM | 0.504 |
| pLDDT | 0.356 |
| `min_ipae`, CDRH3↔HER2 | 0.497 |
| `mean_ipae`, CDRH3↔HER2 | 0.454 |
| **sequence-only `p_binder`** | **0.849** |

**Every fold metric is at chance.** Among the 30 binders, no fold metric tracks
KD at all (|Spearman| ≤ 0.21, p ≥ 0.28), while the sequence ranker reaches
+0.687 under grouped CV. Adding fold metrics to the sequence features made the
classifier **worse** (0.849 → 0.806).

**ipTM ≥ 0.7 passes 100% of true non-binders.** As a gate it removes nothing.

## Why we think it happened

All 60 complexes share **326 of 336 tokens** — only the 10 CDRH3 residues vary —
so global confidence is dominated by the unchanged framework and HER2. ipTM
spans just 0.070 across the whole panel.

That explanation does **not** cover `min_ipae`, which has a real spread of 3.0
and still sits at chance. So this is not a resolution artefact: the interface
error estimate genuinely varies and genuinely fails to predict binding.

## What it implies for the theories

- **TH-013 strengthened, and its compromise clause corrected.** TH-013's
  `evidence_against` said a structure score was "still worth keeping as a
  *filter* that discards the clearly bad". Measured, that is false for this
  track: ipTM ≥ 0.7 passes every true non-binder. Corrected in place.
- **TH-013 scope sharpened.** The negative applies to **fixed-framework CDRH3
  optimisation**. For de novo, where the whole binder changes, AF3 ipTM retains
  signal (AUROC 0.75, EXP-003). Two regimes, two answers.
- **TH-011 confirmed again**, now on the metric rather than on loop geometry.
- **IDEA-012 vindicated.** The decoy control cost ~8 minutes of H100 and
  overturned the basis of our ranking. It belongs in the pipeline as a rule.

**Honest limit:** n=60 rules out a strong effect (AUROC > ~0.65). It does not
rule out a weak one.

## Operationally useful by-product

Fold cost measured on our own H100: **7.9 s per structure** (475 s for 60),
including container and model load. The Nebius `START-HERE.md` estimate of ~80 s
per fold is wrong by ~10× for this setup. Refolding is far cheaper than we
assumed — which is a reason to use it as a plausibility check on submissions,
not a reason to screen with it.

## What we would do differently

Run the decoy control before adopting a metric, not after ranking on it. EXP-001
ranked two designs on a 0.021 min_ipae gap; this experiment shows min_ipae does
not distinguish binders from non-binders at all on this track.
