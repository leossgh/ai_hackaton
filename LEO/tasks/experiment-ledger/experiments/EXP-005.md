# EXP-005 — thermostability: NanoMelt is the tool, and CDRH3 cannot move Tm

## What we expected

That a usable thermostability predictor exists for our designs, and that CDRH3
redesign — the thing all four HER2 weights do — affects thermostability. Both
mattered because Tm is one of three judged properties and the **only one with no
labelled data in the hackathon set**.

## What actually happened

### The tooling question is settled

**Neither platform ships a Tm predictor.** Nebius' `stab-ddg` is StaB-ddG, which
predicts **binding** ddG, not folding stability. Foundry's "AQ" workflows are
SandboxAQ small-molecule tools.

**Cheap features cannot substitute.** On NanoMelt's own 639 measurements,
composition/charge/hydrophobicity features reach Pearson +0.40, MAE 7.6 °C,
against 8.3 °C for simply predicting the mean.

**NanoMelt is the usable tool** (mAbs 2025; 639 measured nanobody Tm; published
held-out Pearson 0.853, MAE 4.1 °C). Installed on the VM and **verified to
reproduce its own reference output exactly**. ~45 sequences/s on the H100.

### CDRH3 is thermostability-inert, and there is a control

41 variants of the identical 120-aa trastuzumab VH differing **only** in the
10-residue CDRH3 (10 tightest binders, 10 weakest, 10 non-measurable,
hydrophobicity extremes, one engineered Cys pair):

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Predicted Tm spread, whole panel | **4.63 °C** (std 0.96) |
| Variants beyond the 4.1 °C MAE | **0 of 40** |
| Spearman(predicted Tm, measured KD) | **+0.001** |

The entire spread sits inside the predictor's error bar. The control rules out
"the model is simply blind" — single point mutations in the same VH:

| Region | n | mean ΔTm | range | beyond 4.1 °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Framework | 57 | −0.76 °C | −6.96 to +2.05 | 3 |
| CDRH3 | 20 | −0.09 °C | −1.30 to +0.56 | 0 |

|ΔTm| median 0.63 vs 0.19 °C, Mann-Whitney **p = 0.002**. The model resolves
point mutations; CDRH3 just does not move Tm.

**Independent sanity check:** the scan rediscovers the disulfide effect
unprompted. `C22Q` (removing a canonical cysteine) is predicted at −5.03 °C, and
NanoMelt's own training data has mean Tm 41 °C at 0 cysteines, 67 °C at 2, 73 °C
at 4, dropping to 53 °C at 5 (odd ⇒ unpaired). Even counts stabilise, odd
destabilise — which is also exactly what `abguard`'s no-unpaired-cysteine rule
encodes.

### Framework gains are real but small and awkward

Full non-CDR saturation: every position × 19 substitutions = **1,577 single
mutants**, 35 s of compute.

- mean ΔTm **−0.59 °C**; only **27.6% stabilising at all**; **0 above +4.1 °C**; 19 below −4.1 °C
- best single: `R98A` **+3.74 °C** — still inside the error bar
- most fragile, do not touch: W36, C22, C96, L20, M83, V48, D73, E89

Combinations are **sub-additive and then harmful**: stack3 reaches +4.28 °C where
additivity predicts +6.23; stack12 collapses to **+1.44 °C** where additivity
predicts +18.60 °C. Greedy ordering is not optimal — random 6-mutation subsets
average +4.16 °C, best +6.09 °C, and 12 of 29 combinations clear +4.1 °C.

### Three traps in the framework hits

- **Charge bias.** 11 of the top 12 single mutations are **→ Glu**, the only substitution with a positive mean effect across the whole scan. NanoMelt appears to have learned "more acidic = more stable", so stacked hits are largely a big net-charge change, with unknown cost to expression and function.
- **`V37F` is a VHH hallmark position.** The model partly wants to *nanobody-ify* trastuzumab's VH — a training-domain artefact, not evidence about stabilising a VH/VL pair.
- **Lone cysteines.** 4 of the top 20 single mutations introduce an unpaired Cys, which `abguard` rejects outright. Filtered, with the cysteine count asserted constant.
- **Position 98** sits at the `...YYC-S-R|WGGDGFYAMD` junction, immediately N-terminal to CDRH3, dominates the top hits, and is the most likely place to disturb antigen binding. Also filtered.

## What it implies for the theories

- **TH-005 confirmed, `medium` → `high`.** "Put binding risk in CDRH3 and buy stability back in the framework" is now backed by a controlled result rather than one paper: CDRH3 is inert, framework is not, p = 0.002.
- **TH-007 retired.** Its claim was that *scFv* thermostability is predictable well enough to rank, closing the open decision. The only usable tool is nanobody-trained and shows domain artefacts on a VH. Superseded by TH-016.
- **TH-016 (new, `high`).** NanoMelt is the tool; Tm is rankable in-domain for a VHH and out-of-domain for a VH/scFv.
- **TH-017 (new, `medium`).** Framework stabilisation is real but bounded: no single mutation clears the error bar, stacking collapses, and the best hits carry charge, cysteine and position-98 artefacts.
- **TH-012 reinforced.** NanoMelt being sdAb-trained is a third leg under "a VHH is better supported by our tooling".
- **IDEA-003 done.** The predictor decision is answered.

## What we would do differently

Nothing about the method — the control design is the model to copy. The thing to
carry forward is that a predictor's **training domain** is part of its error bar:
NanoMelt's MAE of 4.1 °C is for nanobodies, and on a VH the artefacts (→Glu,
V37F) are the model reaching for its own domain.
